5 steps to a conversation

Step 1: Introduction

Break the ice and build rapport with your BEAST factors: Body language, Eye contact, Awesome attitude, Smile, Tonality/timing.

  • Smile with open body language, like greeting a friend.
  • Open with a genuine compliment: "Hey, how are you doing, sir/ma'am?" "Cool shirt!"
  • Use your outside voice, especially in loud stores like Costco.
  • Turn & Burn: grab your board and approach swiftly.
Step 2: Short story (the 1st 5s)

Explain why you're there and what you're doing to reduce skepticism and uncover needs.

  • Who is your cell phone provider? (Power word: "Perfect!")
  • How many lines do you have with them?
  • What kinds of phones?
  • How much are you paying?
  • Is anyone in the household a teacher, nurse, military, or first responder?
  • 4.5: Since the T-Mobile merge with Sprint/Boost/Metro, have you experienced dropped calls?
  • 5: What has (provider) done to help you with (pain bucket)?

Four pain buckets: Phones, Data, Coverage, Price. Use CPR (create personal relationships) with FORDS topics: Family, Occupation, Recreation, Dreams, School/Sports.

Step 3: Presentation (the 2nd 5s)
  • Unlimited with 5G access, AT&T as the #1 carrier
  • Compare their equipment to what you will provide (use tonality)
  • Pain buckets and perks: premium data, 5G+ coverage, hotspot
  • Rehash, then price: review everything discussed, then tag it with the price
  • Value statement: "I'm assuming this is going to be of value to you."

After the second value statement, go straight for your iPad. If they follow, you have built enough impulse.

Step 4: Hard close (the 3rd 5s)
  • Turn and Burn: start the credit application. "All I need is your driver's license to see if you qualify."
  • Tester question: phone color, type of phones, "keep the same number, right?"
  • Hard close: "Let's do it." Assume the sale.
  • Pre-empt: tell them the next step. People don't mind negatives, they hate surprises.
  • Rehash: bill breakdown. Great time for referrals.

While processing, stick to CPR and FORDS, not product talk. Ask the customer to pull up their current account number and PIN. Tell them what you need rather than asking if they're ready.

Step 5: Rehash and set expectations

Set expectations only after the customer accepts the terms and conditions.

  • All promotions take 3 billing cycles to apply. Trade-in credit requires returning the old device.
  • 1st bill: $30 activation per line, month of service, and normal phone finance charge per line.
  • 2nd bill: month of service and normal finance charge.
  • 3rd bill: credits for 3 months of device payments (if the phone was sent back right away), so it will be much lower and hard to predict.
  • 4th bill: should be normal, possibly lower from credits.
  • 5th bill: the price quoted in store.

Objections

Objections are good. People usually object because they're confused. Never panic.
Early (intro/short story): AIR = Acknowledge, Ignore, Resume. If they say no a second time, say "No problem, just really quick, do you have (two competitor options)?" and then wish them a great day.
Late (presentation/close): FFF = Feel, Felt, Found: "I understand how you feel. Previous customers felt the same way, but what they found was…"
Objection triangle
  1. Acknowledge: "I completely understand how you feel." / "I hear what you're saying."
  2. Overcome: offer a solution. "Here's what I can do for you…"
  3. Resume: "With that being said…"

Customer types (BOLT)

Bull direct

Results oriented, wants no small talk, may seem rude because they don't want to be sold to.

  • Mirror their energy, stay calm
  • High indifference, be transparent
  • Move fast, limit story and Jones
Owl questions

Wants details, stories, and full explanations. Dislikes doubt words and overexcitement.

  • High urgency, but steady pace
  • Stick to your system
  • Stay cool, preempt each step
Lamb agreeable

Calm, social, slow to decide, changes their mind.

  • Be very clear and directive
  • Social first, then business
  • Use sense of urgency
Tiger excited

Loves a deal, decides fast, few objections. Future talk shows high impulse.

  • Go straight to the hard close
  • Use fear of loss
  • Check they qualify

8 great work habits

  1. Have and maintain a great attitude. Take rejection in stride.
  2. Be on time. Early is on time.
  3. Be prepared. Plan for every situation.
  4. Work a full day. Follow through until success.
  5. Work your store/department correctly. Read the weekly ad to find "hot spots."
  6. Respect the customer and client. Stay positive, never criticize.
  7. Understand your opportunity. Know your purpose.
  8. Take control. Of the situation, the outcome, and your future.

STOP sign cues

When you hear or see these, stop and move to the next step: Just moved, Reverse Jones, Service-based questions, "Oh wow, really?", Love/Hate, Body language, Silence, Future talk.

Theories

Swan, Bumble Bee, QVC
  • Swan: stay calm in front of customers, even if you don't know the answer. They feed off your energy. If unsure, say you'll "quickly verify with my manager."
  • Bumble Bee: know and sell all products to all types of people.
  • QVC: use all your impulse factors with every customer throughout the pitch.
Bamboo, Gumball, Law of Averages, Bullet
  • Bamboo: bamboo grows roots for years, then sprouts 90 feet in weeks. Build your foundation first.
  • Gumball: invest all your time in one machine. Replicate your system and do the ordinary extraordinarily.
  • Law of Averages: the more customers you talk to, the more likely you close.
  • Bullet: have a prepared answer for every objection. Don't reveal every promotion at once. Hold one back (such as a gift card) for a customer who hasn't committed.
KISS and KILL

KISS (Keep It Short & Simple): hit the key points and close. KILL (Keep It Long & Lengthy): not our standard. For customers with more lines, or who need extra time, slow down and be patient, but don't push them into feeling cornered.
